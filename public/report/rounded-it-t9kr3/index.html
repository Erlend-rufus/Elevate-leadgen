02 Accuracy

Google knows you, and it has told us precisely what is missing

This section is not a measure of visibility. Assistants answer at length about almost any named business with a website. What matters is what the answer says and which sources it was built from.

“Rounded IT (roundedit.co.uk) is an entry-level UK-based managed service provider (MSP) that serves as a highly convenient all-in-one option if your business needs basic outsourced IT management bundled directly with broadband and connectivity solutions.”

Worth watching

“Vague Cybersecurity Specifics: While they state that they apply strong data security and proactive monitoring, their public documentation lacks explicit mentions of structural compliance framework alignments (such as being a certified Microsoft Solutions Partner, or holding explicit Cyber Essentials Plus or ISO 27001 accreditations).”

“Limited Online Client Footprint: Independent public reviews and peer evaluations specifically for roundedit.co.uk are incredibly sparse across major aggregation channels. They should not be confused with similarly named entities, such as the spare parts vendor Rounded.com or East Midlands-based RoundWorks IT.”

Google AI Mode, brand test, 26 August 2026. Verbatim. All three sentences are visible in the fourth capture below.

Put the second quote next to the Cyber Essentials answer and the whole audit resolves into one sentence. Google says accreditation is what qualifies a provider for that answer, and Google says your public documentation does not mention yours. Whatever certifications Rounded IT holds, they are not written where machines are reading.

The word entry-level is worth pausing on too. Your site leads with managed IT and broadband tailored for business, 100 per cent UK-based support, and coverage from London to West Yorkshire. Google turned that into entry-level and relatively generalized. That is not an insult, it is a description of what can be verified: no named accreditations, no named client work, no independent reviews.

The third quote is a separate risk. Google raised, unprompted, that Rounded IT could be confused with Rounded.com and with RoundWorks IT. Name overlap in a crowded category means attribution leaks to whoever has the clearer public record.

One thing worth noting in your favour. The bundling of broadband and IT support in one contract is the thing Google understood best, and it is the thing the West Yorkshire answer was specifically looking for. The proposition lands. The evidence behind it does not exist yet in a form a machine can quote.

03 Gaps

Four reasons the answers skip you

Each of these is tied to something observed above, not to general best practice.

1

No accreditations stated where machines can read them

Every provider named in the Cyber Essentials answer was named because of a specific credential: IASME Certification Body, NCSC-assured Cyber Advisor, Cyber Essentials Plus assessor, Microsoft Solutions Partner. Google told us directly that your documentation lacks explicit mentions of these. If you hold any of them, they are the single cheapest thing to publish.

2

No independent review footprint

Google called independent public reviews for roundedit.co.uk incredibly sparse across major aggregation channels. Competitors in these answers carry review volume on Google Business Profiles and industry directories. A provider with no third-party record has nothing for an engine to weigh.

3

No machine-readable corporate identity

The pages we fetched carry no company registration number and no structured data. There is nothing that binds the website, the company record and any review profile into one verified entity, which is what makes the confusion with Rounded.com and RoundWorks IT possible in the first place.

4

No regional proof page for the area you actually cover

The West Yorkshire answer named five providers, several of them explicitly located in Leeds, Halifax and Bradford. Your site says London to West Yorkshire and beyond. A general statement of coverage is not the same as a page that says what you do in that region, in the words a buyer there would use.

Already working for you: a clear proposition that Google understood correctly and described accurately, namely managed IT and business broadband under one contract, which is exactly what the regional question was looking for. Google also picked up your uptime commitment and the depth of experience your site claims. The message is landing. What is missing is the evidence layer underneath it.

04 The work

What we would do, and what each fix changes

Ordered by effect against effort. The left is the work, the right is what a machine can do afterwards that it cannot do today.

1

Publish every accreditation you hold, by name

Cyber Essentials, Cyber Essentials Plus, IASME, Microsoft Partner status, ISO certifications, each with the issuing body and the year, on a page of their own and in the footer.

You become quotable in the exact answer where Google said accreditation decides the outcome.

2

Build an independent review footprint

Google Business Profile reviews from existing clients first, then the directories these answers cite.

There is third-party evidence to weigh, rather than only your own claims.

3

Publish the legal identity and add entity markup

Registered company name and number, address, and Organization markup with sameAs links to your review and directory profiles.

Rounded IT resolves to one verifiable entity, and the confusion with Rounded.com and RoundWorks IT loses its technical basis.

4

Write regional and use-case proof pages

One page for West Yorkshire and one for the broadband and IT bundle, in plain text, using the vocabulary the answers used: response time SLAs, no long lock-ins, all-inclusive security, single supplier for connectivity and support.

Engines quote plain text. You control the sentence they lift into a regional answer.

07 Method

How this was measured

All queries were run manually in a browser on 26 August 2026, in English, phrased the way a business owner would ask rather than as keywords.

Engine: Google AI Mode with UK region and language settings. Three category questions and one brand test. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot were not run in this round.

The exact URLs, so the measurement can be repeated word for word: google.com/search?udm=50&hl=en-GB&gl=GB with q set to "Which managed IT service providers should a UK small business use?", "We need managed IT support and business broadband in West Yorkshire, which company should we contact?", "Which UK IT support companies are best for a small business that needs Cyber Essentials certification?" and "Is Rounded IT (roundedit.co.uk) a good managed IT provider?".

Each capture shows the answer as returned, at reduced page zoom so that nothing quoted sits outside the image. Every provider name and every sentence quoted in this report is visible in the capture it is attributed to.

Queries were run from Norway with UK language and region settings, so answers to region-specific questions may differ slightly for a buyer physically in the UK. The session was signed in to a Google account, so personalisation cannot be fully excluded.

One answer is one data point, not a verdict. Repeating these queries will produce some variation, which is why any re-measurement uses the same wording.

Website facts were taken from the live pages at roundedit.co.uk the same day: the page title reads Rounded IT, Managed IT and Broadband Solutions, the main heading reads Managed IT and Broadband Solutions Tailored for Business, and coverage is described as from London to West Yorkshire and beyond with 100 per cent UK-based support. We found no company registration number and no structured data on the pages fetched.
