7. Your rights

You can ask us at any time to:

  • show you the details we hold about you, and give you a copy
  • correct details that are wrong
  • delete your details, where we have no good reason to keep them
  • limit how we use your details while a question about them is being settled
  • give you your details in a form a computer can read, or send them to another business, where that right applies
  • stop using your details for marketing. We will always do this.
  • stop using your details where we rely on our own legitimate interests, unless we can show a strong reason to carry on
  • stop using details you let us use because you consented, for example the Meta measurement in Part 9
  • have a person look again at how your application was routed

To ask, write to post@elevatemarketing.no. We answer within one month. If a request is complicated, the law lets us take longer, and we will tell you why. We may need to check who you are first. We do not charge for this, except in the narrow cases the law allows.

If you think we have handled your details wrongly, please tell us first so that we can put it right. You can also complain to the regulator. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). In Norway, where we are based, it is Datatilsynet (datatilsynet.no).

9. Meta and the measurement of our advertising

If you choose Accept, two things happen. The Meta pixel on our pages tells Meta which pages you saw. And when your application is one we take forward, our server sends Meta a short message so that Meta can match it to the advertisement you clicked. That message holds scrambled (hashed) versions of your email address, first name and last name, your IP address, your browser details, and the identifiers Meta set when you clicked. We send no phone number. We use this only to see which advertisements lead to real applications, and to improve who sees our advertising.

If you choose Decline, none of this is sent, and we do not keep the advertising identifiers with your application.

What Meta does when it shows you our advertisement is covered by Meta's own privacy policy. Meta also decides for itself how it uses some of what it receives.
