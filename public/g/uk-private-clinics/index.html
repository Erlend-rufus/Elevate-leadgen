Three things we do not do

We do not lead with Meta.

Meta classifies clinics as a health and wellness data source and restricts what its algorithm is allowed to learn from your patients. Campaigns that depend on it get worse quietly. Search is where the intent is, and search is where we start.

We do not touch treatment data.

We are paid on attended consultations, and a monthly number is all we need. We never ask for, receive or process anything about who the patient was or what they came in for.

We do not talk to your patients.

No calls, no chasing, no follow-up sequences in your name. The booking lands in your calendar and the rest is your clinic.

This works if

  • A meaningful share of your revenue is self-pay

  • Patients can book online today, in a system that records whether they attended

  • You could see at least twenty more new patients next month

  • One named clinician can approve campaign material within a couple of days

This does not work if

  • Nearly all your income is insured or contracted volume

  • Booking is by phone only, with no record we can both see

  • You are already at capacity, in which case come back when you are not

  • Nobody clinical has time to sign anything off

How we get paid

We invoice a fixed fee for each consultation that came from a campaign we run and that the patient actually attended. Not per booking. If they do not turn up, there is nothing to invoice and nothing to argue about.

The table at the top of this page counts bookings, because that is what an ad account can see. We invoice on the ones who turn up, which only your clinic can see. We are comfortable being paid on the smaller number.

Your booking system is the record, and an aggregate count is all we need. We never ask who the patient was or what they came in for.

There is no retainer, no setup fee and no minimum term. The fee depends on your speciality and what a consultation is worth to you, so we agree it before anything runs. There is nothing to sign to have the conversation.

Straight answers

Is this a referral fee?

No. A referral fee is money paid to someone who sends you a patient, and that is what the GMC and GDC rules are about. We are paid for advertising that we produce and run, in the same way you would pay any agency, with the amount tied to how well it worked.

What counts as an attended consultation?

A patient who came from a campaign we run, booked, and turned up. Rescheduled appointments count when the patient attends the new time. Cancellations, no-shows and anything you decline to see do not. You send a monthly count from your own system and we invoice against it.

Who owns the ad accounts?

You do. They are opened in your name, you hold the billing, and if we stop working together you keep everything, including the campaign history.

What do you need from us to start?

A named clinician who can approve material, access to your booking figures, and a decision on how many new patients you can take.

What if it does not work?

Then you have paid for the advertising you would have paid for anyway. If nobody turns up, you have paid us nothing at all. That is the whole point of the model, and it is also why we are selective about who we take on.

Are you regulated to give clinical advice?

No, and we do not. Everything clinical is yours.
