Elevate Marketing · Documented case study

What it actually costs to win an IT consultancy client

For nine reported months we rebuilt and ran paid acquisition for Avant IT, an Oslo consultancy specialising in the legal sector. The account produced 214 quote requests for approximately 118,000 NOK of media. Taken with the client's own figures, the media cost of winning a client came to roughly two and a half percent of contract value. Every figure on this page is marked by where it comes from.

01 The client

A technically strong firm with no predictable pipeline

Avant IT AS was an Oslo IT consultancy of 22 people, founded on 1 August 2001, company number 983 591 744. Its specialism was law firms and in-house legal departments, served through the company's own Legal 365 platform, built on Microsoft 365. In October 2024 the company was acquired by Avoki, a Nordic IT group, and in December 2024 it was renamed Avoki IT AS.

If you run a UK IT consultancy, the shape should be familiar. Technically strong, well referred, and with no predictable inbound pipeline. New work arrived because someone knew someone. The Google Ads account existed, but under the previous provider it was an expense rather than a channel.

02 Starting position

Four quote requests a month, at £320 each

When we took the account over in September 2024, its recent baseline under the previous provider was four quote requests a month, at 4,310 NOK each, approximately £320. Clicks cost 12.30 NOK against an industry benchmark of 5.60 NOK cited in the original reports: more than double, for traffic that rarely asked for anything.

A word on terminology, because it matters for everything below

These were quote requests: a prospective client asking to be quoted for work. Not newsletter signups, not contact form messages, not downloads. It is the unit a pipeline is actually made of, and it is the only unit this page counts.

03 What was done

A sequence, run in order

No reinvention. Each stage exists because of the data from the one before.

  1. Measurement first. Conversion tracking and the landing pages were rebuilt so that a quote request meant a quote request, and nothing softer counted as one.
  2. Performance Max, deliberately. Run early to harvest search term data at volume. A means to an end, not a permanent strategy.
  3. Search campaigns built from evidence. The harvested terms became tightly themed search campaigns aimed at the queries that had actually produced quote requests.
  4. Retargeting. Added to stay present with visitors who had not yet asked for a quote. How that ended is in section 06.
  5. Lookalike audiences built from converters, not visitors. The difference between those two audiences is most of the value.

04 The economics

The number you would actually decide on, labelled honestly

It rests partly on figures only the client can state, so it sits in its own section with its own label.

Client supplied figures · not evidenced by Elevate

Everything outside this panel comes from the nine monthly reports, filed accounts or public announcements. This panel is different. The three statements below were made by the client verbally. They do not appear in the reports, we hold no written confirmation, and we cannot evidence them. We publish them because they are the numbers you would actually decide on, and because they are consistent with the documented record. Consistent is not the same as proven. Treat them accordingly.

  • Close to half of the quote requests converted into work.
  • A Legal 365 implementation averages around 50,000 NOK, approximately £3,700.
  • Over the period the company deliberately narrowed, growing Legal 365 while other service lines declined.
~2.5% media cost of winning one client, as a share of contract value

Put plainly: roughly two and a half percent of contract value to win the contract, against approximately 118,000 NOK of media and 214 documented quote requests. That is the figure to hold against your own numbers, because it is how you would judge any other cost of acquisition. We deliberately do not express it as a price per client. The inputs are not evidenced tightly enough for that sentence to survive scrutiny, and scrutiny is exactly what this page is for.

06 The narrow month

The smallest number on the page

July 2025 looks like a failure in the table above. 8,130 impressions against a normal 80,000. It was deliberate: a narrow campaign on a single proposition, security as a service, aimed at one buyer problem.

439 clicks produced 18 quote requests. A conversion rate of 4 percent, roughly eight times the account average of 0.5 percent. Cost per request rose to 833 NOK, and volume fell. We accepted both, on purpose. That trade is the entire argument Elevate makes in every sales conversation: a specific offer to a specific buyer beats a broad one. July is the month that proves we believe it with someone else's money.

Two things we would rather say out loud

First, accuracy. The July campaign sold security as a service, so those 18 requests were not Legal 365 requests. The correct description of the 214 total is quote requests for the client's services, with Legal 365 as the primary proposition across most of the period.

Second, a failure. The July report states plainly that retargeting produced no conversions that month. Lookalike audiences built from converters did. We include this because it is true, and because an account in which everything works is an account you are not being told about. Retargeting was not the right tool for a narrow, high intent offer. We learned that on the client's budget, in one bounded month, and it is now part of how we judge the next account.

07 The acquisition

Framed honestly

Two weeks into the engagement's second month, Avoki announced it had acquired Avant IT. Let us be precise about what that does and does not mean.

  • 1 August 2001Avant IT AS founded in Oslo.
  • September 2024First monthly report of the engagement. The account is rebuilt as described in section 03.
  • 2 October 2024Avoki announces the acquisition of Avant IT.
  • 12 December 2024The company is renamed Avoki IT AS.
  • Into 2025The account keeps running through the integration and into the merged group, for a total of nine reported months.

We did not cause the acquisition, and the dates make that impossible. Acquisitions of this kind are negotiated for months before they are announced. Our first report predates the announcement by one month. Any page implying otherwise would collapse the moment you checked, and this audience checks.

What happened next is the credential. Acquirers review supplier contracts; it is one of the first things a new owner does. Avoki reviewed this one and kept it running through the integration and into the group. Being retained through an acquisition is a harder credential than causing one. Anyone can claim the second. The first requires the new owner's accountants to agree with you.

09 The call

Thirty minutes about your numbers, not our pitch

If you run an IT consultancy of 10 to 50 people and your pipeline still depends on referrals, this page was written for you. The next step is not a proposal and not a pitch. It is thirty minutes about your numbers: what a client is worth to you, what you have tried, and what paid acquisition would have to cost to make sense.

Sometimes the honest answer is that it is not worth your money yet. We say so. Elevate works on a retainer plus commission, so our incentives point the same way as yours, and we take on a small number of IT consultancies at a time.
