Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is the practice of making your business easy for AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews to find, understand and cite when they answer questions. Where SEO competes for a place in a list of links, GEO competes for a place inside the answer itself.

## Why is there a new discipline at all?

Because a growing share of buying research now happens inside AI tools rather than on a results page. In October 2025, OpenAI's chief executive announced that ChatGPT had reached [800 million weekly active users](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/06/sam-altman-says-chatgpt-has-hit-800m-weekly-active-users/). And in February 2024, the analysis firm [Gartner predicted](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-02-19-gartner-predicts-search-engine-volume-will-drop-25-percent-by-2026-due-to-ai-chatbots-and-other-virtual-agents) that traditional search engine volume would drop 25% by 2026, as people move questions to AI chatbots and other virtual agents.

That Gartner figure is a forecast, not a measurement, and reasonable people can disagree about the pace of the shift. The direction, though, is not seriously disputed: more and more of your potential clients now ask an assistant "who should I use for this?" before they type anything into Google at all. If the assistant has never heard of you, you are not part of that conversation.

GEO is the practical response to that shift.

## How AI assistants decide what to say

Two mechanisms matter, and they work differently.

**The model's memory.** Large language models are trained on enormous snapshots of the public web. Whatever they absorbed about your business during training sits inside the model as background knowledge. You cannot edit this directly, and it refreshes on the provider's schedule, not yours.

**Live retrieval.** Increasingly, assistants also search the web in real time before answering, and then cite the pages they used. OpenAI documents [three separate crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) covering training, search and user-triggered fetching, and all of them respect the standard robots.txt rules on your website.

The second mechanism is where GEO does most of its work. When an assistant fetches fresh pages to build an answer, it tends to favour sources that are:

- **Direct.** The question is answered plainly, early, in words a person would actually use.
- **Specific.** Concrete services, locations and details rather than vague claims about passion and excellence.
- **Consistent.** The same business name, address, phone number and description everywhere the business appears.
- **Corroborated.** Independent sites — reviews, directories, local press, trade bodies — say similar things about you.

In other words, the assistant assembles an answer the way a careful researcher would. It looks for material that is easy to read, easy to trust and easy to check against other sources.

## GEO vs SEO: what changes and what does not

The reassuring news first: GEO is not a replacement for SEO. Google's own [guidance on AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) states that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond the normal ones — your pages must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet. The same foundations feed both surfaces: fast pages, clear structure, content written to genuinely help the reader.

What changes is the unit you compete for:

| Traditional SEO | GEO |
|---|---|
| A ranking in a list of links | A mention inside a generated answer |
| Measured in positions and clicks | Measured in citations and mentions |
| Rewards the page that wins the click | Rewards the page that answers the question |

You can rank first on Google and still be absent from an assistant's answer, because the assistant is not copying the results page. It is synthesising its own answer from several sources, and it only cites the ones that gave it something usable.

## What you can do yourself, starting this week

A serious first pass costs nothing but time:

1. **Ask the machines.** Open ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini, and ask the questions a real potential client would ask: "Who are the best employment solicitors in Manchester?" or "How do I choose an accountant for a small limited company?" Note who gets mentioned and whether you appear at all.
2. **Check the doors are open.** Look at your robots.txt file and make sure you are not accidentally blocking AI crawlers. It happens more often than you would think, usually as a side effect of an old security setting.
3. **Rewrite one service page as a set of answers.** Take your most important service and structure the page around the questions clients actually ask: what it is, who it is for, what it involves and what happens next.
4. **Tidy your public footprint.** Search your own company name. Old addresses, old phone numbers and half-finished directory profiles all blur the picture an assistant builds of you.
5. **Earn independent mentions.** A trade association listing, a piece in local press, genuine client reviews on a recognised platform. Assistants trust what others say about you far more than what you say about yourself.

## What GEO cannot do

A few honest limits, because the hype around this topic is considerable.

Nobody can guarantee that an assistant will cite you. There is no paid placement for the organic answers, no submission form and no switch to flip. What you can do is steadily raise the odds: be reachable, be clear, be corroborated. Results also lag. The model's memory updates on the provider's timetable, so some improvements take time to show up even after you have made them.

Finally, GEO cannot fix a weak underlying offer. If your service page would not convince a human reader, it will not convince a machine either. Any provider who guarantees you a top spot in ChatGPT's answers is promising something no one controls.

## Where GEO fits in a sensible marketing plan

Think of GEO as a layer on top of work that should already be happening: sound technical SEO, pages that answer real questions, a steady flow of genuine reviews, and a website that converts the visitors it gets. For most service businesses, the right order is to fix those foundations first and then tune for AI visibility — not to buy GEO as a detached add-on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GEO just SEO with a new name?

The foundations overlap heavily: crawlability, clear structure and genuinely useful content matter in both. The difference is the target — a mention inside a generated answer rather than a position in a list — and how success is measured. Be sceptical of anyone selling GEO as an entirely separate, expensive discipline whilst your site's basic SEO remains broken.

### Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT's answers?

No. There is currently no advertising product that buys placement inside the organic, generated answers. The only route in is to be a source the assistant considers worth citing: clear, consistent and corroborated by others.

### How do I know whether AI assistants mention my business?

Ask them. Once a month, run a fixed set of ten realistic customer queries through the major assistants and record who gets mentioned. That simple log is your baseline metric, and it will show movement over time far more honestly than any vanity report.
