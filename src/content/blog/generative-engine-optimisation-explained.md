Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) is the practice of making sure AI assistants — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot and Google's AI Overviews — describe and recommend your business accurately when buyers ask. Where SEO earns you a position in a list of links, GEO earns you a place inside the answer itself.

## Why this matters now

More and more buyers start their research by asking an AI assistant instead of typing keywords into a search box. They ask full questions — "which accountant should a small limited company use in Manchester?" — and they get one synthesised answer, not ten blue links. If your business is not part of that answer, the enquiry goes to whoever is.

The uncomfortable part: AI assistants do not wait for your permission. They already have an opinion about your business, assembled from whatever sources they can find. That description can be out of date, incomplete, or simply wrong — and most business owners have never checked it.

## GEO and SEO: related, not the same

The two disciplines overlap, but they optimise for different systems.

- **SEO** optimises for a ranked list: keywords, backlinks, technical health, page experience. Success is a position.
- **GEO** optimises for the answer itself: the sources AI systems cite, the facts they repeat, the entities they recognise, and the structured signals that make your business legible to a language model. Success is being named, described correctly and recommended.

Good SEO helps GEO — authoritative, crawlable content feeds both. But a site can rank well on Google and still be invisible to AI assistants, because the assistants lean on different signals: consistent business data across the web, citations from sources they trust, clear entity information, and content that answers questions directly.

## What GEO work actually involves

In practice, GEO for a service business comes down to four areas:

1. **Visibility** — finding out where you appear today when buyers ask AI assistants about your service and area, and where you are absent.
2. **Accuracy** — correcting what the assistants get wrong: retired services, old addresses, wrong specialisms.
3. **Gaps** — identifying which sources the assistants rely on for your market, and which of them you are missing from.
4. **Fixes** — a prioritised list of changes: citations to earn, business data to make consistent, content to publish, and structured data to add.

None of this is exotic. Most of it is disciplined housekeeping applied to a new distribution channel.

## How to tell whether you need it

Open ChatGPT or Copilot and ask the questions your customers would ask — "best [your service] in [your area]", "who should we use for [your specialism]". Do it in a private window so your own history does not colour the answers.

If you appear, and the description is accurate: good. Check again quarterly. If you are absent while competitors are named, or the description is wrong, that is a visibility problem worth fixing — and the earlier you start, the less catching up you have to do.

## FAQ

### Is GEO just SEO with a new name?

No. They share foundations, but GEO targets how AI systems assemble answers rather than how search engines rank pages. A business can do well at one and poorly at the other.

### Can you guarantee my business appears in AI answers?

No — and be wary of anyone who says otherwise. AI systems change constantly and nobody controls their output. What you can control is the quality and consistency of the signals they draw on.

### How do I check what AI says about my business?

Ask the assistants the questions your buyers would ask, in a private browsing window, and note what comes back. Repeat across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot — the answers often differ, because they draw on different sources.
