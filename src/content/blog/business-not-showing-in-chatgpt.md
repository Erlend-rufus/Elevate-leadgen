If ChatGPT never mentions your business, the cause is usually one of four things: its systems cannot reach your website, your site does not answer real customer questions clearly, your business details are inconsistent across the web, or too few independent sources mention you. All four are checkable, and all four can be fixed.

## Why this matters now

ChatGPT is no longer a novelty. OpenAI announced [800 million weekly active users](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/06/sam-altman-says-chatgpt-has-hit-800m-weekly-active-users/) in October 2025, and a meaningful slice of those people use it the way they once used a search engine: to shortlist suppliers, compare options and sense-check decisions before spending money.

When someone asks "who are the most reliable bathroom fitters in Sheffield?" the assistant does not show ten blue links. It writes an answer, and that answer names a handful of businesses. The question for you is simple: are you in it?

## How ChatGPT decides which businesses to mention

It helps to understand the two ways an assistant knows anything.

**Memory from training.** The underlying model was trained on a vast snapshot of the public web. If your business was rarely mentioned, mentioned inconsistently, or only ever described in vague marketing language, there is little useful material in that memory.

**Live search and citation.** For many questions, ChatGPT now searches the web in real time and cites the pages it relied on. OpenAI documents [three crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots): one for training, one for search and one for fetching pages a user asks about. Site owners can allow or block each of them through robots.txt.

When it assembles an answer, the assistant behaves like a cautious researcher. [Pew Research Center analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) of tens of thousands of real searches found that Google's AI summaries cited three or more sources in 88% of cases, and that the most frequently cited sources were community-built, heavily cross-checked places such as Wikipedia, YouTube and Reddit. The lesson generalises: assistants lean on information that is corroborated in several independent places.

Notice what is absent from that picture: clever tricks. There is no hidden tag, no magic file, no paid doorway into the answer. The businesses that get mentioned are the ones the machine can verify. That is why the four causes below account for almost every case of invisibility.

## The four most common reasons you are invisible

### 1. The crawlers cannot reach you

This is the most mechanical cause and the easiest to miss. A robots.txt rule added years ago, a security plugin, or a CDN setting can block AI crawlers without blocking Google. If the crawler cannot fetch your pages, the assistant has nothing fresh to cite. OpenAI's [crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) tells you exactly which user agents to look for in your configuration.

### 2. Your pages describe you, not the customer's question

Most service business websites open with "Welcome to Smith & Co, a leading provider of…". An assistant cannot do anything with that. It is looking for extractable answers: what you do, who you do it for, where you do it and what the process looks like. Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) makes the same point from the search side: content created primarily to help the reader is what their systems are designed to reward.

### 3. Your details do not match across the web

If Companies House says one address, your website says another, and three directories have an old phone number, an assistant cross-checking sources loses confidence. Inconsistent names, old branding and duplicate listings all blur the picture. Machines, like people, trust what they can verify.

### 4. Nobody else talks about you

This is the hardest one to hear. Assistants weigh what independent sources say far above what you say about yourself. If you have no reviews on recognised platforms, no directory listings, no trade body membership and no local press mentions, the assistant has no corroboration, and an uncorroborated business is a risky recommendation. The fix is unglamorous: ask satisfied clients for reviews consistently, claim the profiles that matter in your trade, and say yes to local visibility when it is offered.

## How to check your own visibility in one sitting

You do not need any tools to establish your baseline:

1. Write down ten questions a real potential client would ask. Use their language, not your industry's: "how much does it cost to…", "who is the best… near me", "do I need a… for…".
2. Run each question through ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Note which businesses get named and which sources get cited.
3. Search your own company name in each assistant. Does it describe you accurately?
4. Check your robots.txt for the crawler names in OpenAI's documentation.
5. Search your company name on Google and click through every profile on the first two pages. Note every inconsistency.

Keep the notes. Repeat the exercise monthly, and you have an honest visibility metric that no agency can spin. If you want it done systematically across all the major platforms, our [free growth audit](/growth-audit) runs precisely this check and reports the findings in plain English.

One warning on method: assistants personalise and vary their answers, so a single search proves nothing either way. What you are looking for is a pattern across several questions and several sessions: never mentioned, mentioned sometimes, or mentioned consistently. That pattern, not any individual answer, is your true starting position.

## What to fix first

Work in this order, because each step depends on the last:

1. **Access.** Unblock the crawlers. Nothing else matters until the assistants can actually read your site.
2. **Answers.** Rewrite your key service pages around the questions clients genuinely ask, with the answer in the first paragraph of each section.
3. **Consistency.** Reconcile your name, address, phone number and description across your website, Companies House, Google Business Profile and the directories that matter in your trade.
4. **Corroboration.** Build genuine reviews and independent mentions. This is the slowest step, and the one that compounds most.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does ChatGPT use Google's search results?

No. ChatGPT can search the web, but it does not copy Google's rankings. It selects and synthesises its own sources, which is why a business can rank well on Google and still never be mentioned in an answer.

### Should I block AI crawlers to protect my content?

That is a trade-off. OpenAI's documentation lets you block training, search or both. Blocking keeps your content out of AI answers, which some publishers want, but for a service business that depends on being found, blocking is usually self-defeating.

### Can I submit my site to ChatGPT?

There is no submission process. Visibility comes from being crawlable, answering real questions clearly, keeping your details consistent and earning independent mentions. Anything claiming to "register" your site with AI assistants is selling something that does not exist.
