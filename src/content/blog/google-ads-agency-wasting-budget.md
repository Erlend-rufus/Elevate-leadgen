You can tell whether a Google Ads agency is wasting your budget with four checks you can run yourself: does conversion tracking measure real enquiries, does the search terms report show irrelevant queries, do reports lead with leads rather than clicks, and do you have full access to your own account. If two or more fail, ask harder questions.

## First: what "working" actually means

A Google Ads account for a service business exists to produce one thing: measurable enquiries: phone calls, form submissions, booked appointments. Clicks, impressions and click-through rates are diagnostics on the way to that outcome, not the outcome itself.

This matters because Google's own automation optimises towards whatever you tell it a conversion is. [Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882) adjusts bids in real time to hit your conversion goals. If the goal is defined badly, with page views counted as leads or no tracking at all, the system will faithfully optimise towards rubbish. Before judging any agency, make sure you know what the account is actually measuring.

## The seven warning signs

### 1. Reports lead with clicks and impressions

If the monthly report opens with traffic charts and buries (or omits) cost per enquiry, the story is being told backwards. Clicks are inputs. You are buying outcomes. Ask to see spend, enquiries and cost per enquiry on the first page, every month, without having to ask twice.

### 2. Conversion tracking is missing or counts the wrong things

Signs: phone calls not tracked, form thank-you pages not measured, or "conversions" that include newsletter clicks and brochure downloads alongside genuine enquiries. An account without clean conversion data cannot be managed properly, and neither can its manager be judged. It is also one of the most common problems in managed accounts, and the cheapest to fix.

### 3. The search terms report is full of irrelevant queries

The search terms report shows the actual queries that triggered your ads. Ask for it. If you are a family solicitor paying for clicks on "free legal advice chat" or "law degree requirements", budget is leaking. A healthy account has a growing negative keyword list precisely because someone reviews this report regularly. An untouched one means nobody is looking.

### 4. Quality components are below average and nobody mentions it

Google rates every keyword on three components (expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience) and rolls them into a 1–10 [Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118). Google describes the score as a diagnostic, not a target, but persistent "below average" components on your highest-spend keywords mean you pay more per click than you need to. If your agency has never shown you these columns, ask why.

### 5. Google's recommendations are applied without judgement

Google surfaces automated recommendations and an [optimisation score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9061546) that largely reflects whether those recommendations have been adopted or dismissed. Some recommendations help; others mainly increase spend. If every suggestion is auto-applied, your account is being steered by the platform that invoices you. Ask which recommendations were accepted last quarter, and why.

### 6. You do not have admin access to your own account

Your Google Ads account, its history and its conversion data belong to you. An agency running campaigns from an account you cannot see leaves you with nothing if you part ways: no history, no learning, no proof. Standard practice is your account, your billing, the agency granted access. Anything less is a structural red flag.

### 7. Nobody can tell you what changed last month

Managed accounts leave fingerprints: new ads tested, keywords added or paused, bids and budgets adjusted, negatives applied. If a month of management fees produced no visible changes, and no explanation of why staying still was the right call, you are paying for supervision, not management.

## How to run the checks yourself

You do not need to be a practitioner. Log into the account (insist on access first), and in one sitting you can:

- Open the search terms report and scan the last 90 days for obviously irrelevant queries.
- Open the conversions section and check what is actually being counted.
- Add the Quality Score component columns to your keyword view and sort by spend.
- Open the change history and see what the agency touched last month.

Any one of these takes minutes. Together they tell you more than a year of polished PDF reports.

A note on what you will find: one irrelevant search term is not a scandal. Auction systems explore, and some waste is the price of reach. What you are judging is the direction of travel: whether the irrelevant share is shrinking because someone is working it, or growing because nobody is.

## The questions to put to your agency

Bring these to your next call and listen to how they are answered:

1. What did you change in the account last month, and what did you learn from it?
2. What exactly counts as a conversion, and can I see the raw list?
3. Which search terms consumed the most budget last month?
4. Which automated recommendations did you accept, and which did you reject?
5. What would you do differently with half the budget, and with double?
6. If we stopped working together tomorrow, what do I keep?

A competent agency will welcome every one of these. Evasion, jargon or offence is an answer in itself.

## What a healthy answer sounds like

For calibration: strong agencies answer these questions concretely and quickly. They name the changes, explain the reasoning in words you can follow, tell you what did not work, and show you the account without being asked twice. Weak answers share a shape too: they retreat to activity ("we optimised the campaigns"), to metrics you did not ask about, or to promises about next month. The difference you are listening for is the difference between evidence and reassurance.

## When to escalate, and when to walk

Raise concerns first. Plenty of mediocre accounts are the product of neglect rather than bad faith, and good agencies respond to informed clients with better work. Set a clear expectation (lead-based reporting, clean tracking, regular search-term reviews) and give it a defined period.

Walk when the structural problems persist: no access, no tracking, no explanations. The account, the data and the budget are yours. ## Frequently asked questions

### Should I have admin access to my own Google Ads account?

Yes, without exception. The account, its billing and its performance history belong to your business. Agencies should be granted access to work in it, never the other way round.

### What should a monthly report include as a minimum?

Spend, number of genuine enquiries, cost per enquiry, the search terms driving spend, and a short list of what changed and why. Everything else is decoration.

### Is Google's optimisation score a good way to judge my agency?

No. The score mainly reflects how Google's automated recommendations have been handled, not whether your account is profitable. An account can score highly and waste money, or score modestly and perform well. Judge on cost per genuine enquiry instead.
